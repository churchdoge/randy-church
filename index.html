The Deacon of Wrigley Field

Randy Church grew up in Hamilton, Illinois, a river town on the shores of the Mississippi, where he learned to track fly balls against the wind coming off the water. He never played a major league game for anyone but the Cubs. Drafted out of Northwestern in 1974, he reached the Cubs the following May and spent fourteen seasons patrolling the deepest center field in the National League.

What set him apart was the mound. Church closed games, made spot starts and, more than once, homered and earned the win on the same afternoon. Managers called him the most valuable roster spot in baseball: an eight-time Gold Glove outfielder who could also get you six outs in the ninth.

He won the National League MVP in 1979 and again in 1984, when his 33 home runs and 121 RBIs carried Chicago to its first postseason in 39 years. The nickname came from his habit of reading the Sunday bulletin in the dugout before day games.

Career highlights

The games people at Wrigley still talk about.

  1. May 14, 1975

    Debuts against the Pirates and triples off the ivy in his first at-bat.

  2. July 22, 1977

    Throws out three runners at the plate in one game, a modern NL record for a center fielder, and wins his first Gold Glove that fall.

  3. August 11, 1979

    Walk-off grand slam in the 11th inning to beat the Cardinals 8–4. The ball cleared Waveland Avenue and was never found.

  4. September 1979

    Hits .330 to win the batting title, adds 34 home runs and 118 RBIs, and takes his first MVP award.

  5. June 5, 1980

    Starts on the mound against the Mets, pitches seven shutout innings and hits a two-run homer in the same game.

  6. July 13, 1982

    All-Star Game MVP in Montreal: a go-ahead double in the 6th and a scoreless 9th inning in relief.

  7. September 24, 1984

    Records the final out in center field as the Cubs clinch the NL East in Pittsburgh. Wins his second MVP.

  8. May 30, 1986

    Second career walk-off grand slam, this time off the Dodgers in the 10th.

  9. October 2, 1988

    Final game. Plays center for eight innings, then pitches a scoreless 9th to a standing ovation.

  10. July 31, 1994

    Inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in Cooperstown on his first ballot.

RANDALL J. CHURCH

CHICAGO N.L. 1975–1988

Rare two-way star who patrolled center field at Wrigley for 14 seasons while doubling as the Cubs’ relief ace. Two-time N.L. Most Valuable Player (1979, 1984) and eight-time Gold Glove winner. Batted .300 lifetime with over 2,200 hits and 300 home runs, and won 51 games on the mound with 52 saves. Hit two walk-off grand slams and led Chicago to the 1984 N.L. East title.

Inducted 1994
